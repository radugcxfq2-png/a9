The Storage Guide

Most damage to a good handbag happens while it is not being used. Creases, colour transfer, stretched handles and mould are all storage problems, and all of them are avoidable.

Empty it, every time

Before a bag goes away for more than a few days, take everything out. Pens leak, cosmetics melt, coins mark the lining and a forgotten snack attracts exactly what you would expect. Turn the bag upside down over a towel, then run a soft brush or a lint roller along the lining seams.

Wipe the outside with a dry, soft cloth. If the leather has been in the rain, let it dry fully at room temperature first. Putting a damp bag in a closed cupboard is how mould begins.

Stuff it lightly

A bag keeps its shape only if something is holding it there. Fill the body with acid-free tissue or a clean cotton pillowcase until the sides are supported but not stretched. Do not use newspaper, because the ink transfers, and do not use plastic bags, which trap moisture.

  • Structured bags need enough filling to stop the top edge folding in.
  • Soft bags need only a little, to stop hard creases forming where they fold.
  • Handles should stand in their natural arch. Wrap long straps loosely and place them inside.
  • Tuck a small piece of tissue between any metal hardware and the leather it rests on.

Use a dust bag, not a box

Leather needs air. A cotton or flannel dust bag keeps off dust and light while letting the leather breathe. A sealed plastic box does the opposite. If the original dust bag is lost, a clean cotton pillowcase is a perfectly good substitute.

Keep light and dark bags in separate dust bags. Dark dye can migrate onto pale leather when two bags sit pressed together for a season.

Where to keep them

The ideal spot is a shelf at eye level, in a room that people live in. Lofts, cellars and garages swing between hot, cold and damp, and leather dislikes all three. Avoid a shelf above a radiator and any place that catches direct sunlight, which fades colour unevenly.

Rotate what you carry

Leather benefits from rest. A bag carried every day for a year wears far faster than the same bag used on alternate weeks, because it never fully dries out or recovers its shape. If you own two everyday bags, swap them weekly. When you swap, take a minute to empty, wipe and stuff the one going back on the shelf.

A check every three months

Set a reminder at the start of each season. Take every stored bag out of its dust bag, look it over in daylight, and let it air for an hour.

  1. Look for pale bloom or spots, the first sign of mould. Wipe with a barely damp cloth and air thoroughly.
  2. Check that tissue has not flattened; refill if the bag has started to slump.
  3. Feel the leather. If it seems dry or stiff, apply a small amount of conditioner made for that type of leather, testing first on a hidden area.
  4. Open and close zips and clasps so they do not seize.

Suede, nubuck and exotic leathers have their own needs and should not be treated with ordinary conditioner. When in doubt, ask a specialist cleaner before applying anything.

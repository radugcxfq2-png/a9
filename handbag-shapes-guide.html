The Shapes & Sizes Guide

There are only a handful of true handbag shapes. Once you know what each one is built to do, choosing between them becomes a practical decision instead of a guess.

Begin with what you carry

Empty your current bag onto a table. Remove anything you have not used in a fortnight. What remains is your real everyday load, and it is usually smaller than you expect: a phone, a wallet, keys, perhaps glasses and a small pouch. The shape you need is the smallest one that holds that pile without forcing it.

A bag that is too big fills up. People rarely carry a half-empty tote; they add a water bottle, a spare jumper and yesterday’s post, and then wonder why their shoulder aches.

The main shapes

Tote

A tote is a tall, open rectangle with two handles long enough to go over the shoulder. It is the natural choice for a laptop or for days that involve carrying papers. Look for a zip or at least a central clasp, a reinforced base and handles stitched through to the inside. An unstructured tote will slump; a little stiffness in the base makes a great difference.

Satchel

The satchel has a flat base, a defined frame and a top handle, usually with a detachable long strap. It holds its shape on a desk and protects what is inside. It is heavier than a soft bag of the same size, so weigh it empty before you decide.

Crossbody

A crossbody spreads weight across the torso and leaves both hands free. It suits travel, crowds and anyone with shoulder trouble. The strap should be adjustable, and the bag should sit at the hip bone, not swing below it.

Bucket

Soft, deep and usually closed with a drawstring. A bucket bag carries bulky things easily, but small items sink to the bottom. Choose one with an interior pocket or add a pouch.

Top-handle and clutch

These are the formal end of the range. A top-handle bag is rigid and compact; a clutch is smaller still. Both are for occasions when you carry very little and want the bag to look deliberate.

Three measurements people forget

Strap drop is the distance from the top of the strap to the top of the bag. Around 22 to 25 cm lets a shoulder bag sit comfortably over a coat. Under 18 cm and it is a hand-carry only.

Base depth decides whether a bag stands up and whether bulky things fit. A depth of 10 cm or more is needed for a water bottle to stand upright.

Empty weight is rarely listed and always matters. Lift the bag before you fill it. If it already feels substantial, it will not improve.

Proportion and the person carrying it

There is no rule that a small person must carry a small bag, but scale does change the effect. A very large tote on a small frame becomes the main event; a tiny bag on a tall frame reads as an accessory. Try the bag in front of a full-length mirror and look at the whole picture, not only the bag.
